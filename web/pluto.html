Dwarf planet

Pluto

The dwarf planet that Clyde Tombaugh found in 1930 by comparing two photographs. SPHEREx caught it here in October 2025, moving on from one stop of the telescope to the next.

The dot that moved in the two photographs was Pluto itself, more than five billion kilometres away. Here the tool is not told where to look: its search for moving dots finds Pluto at every stop of the telescope that caught it, about half an arcsecond from the path NASA computes. And its spectrum carries the mark of methane ice.

What to look for

Each image is one stop of the telescope, in time order. The stars stay where they are; the dot in the ring moves west along the dotted line, the path that NASA’s JPL computes from Pluto’s orbit. Under the images, the tool’s own search for moving dots reports what it found without being told where to look.

SPHEREx has 514 exposures of this spot, from April 25, 2025 to October 4, 2026.
