The mission

What is SPHEREx?

SPHEREx is a NASA space telescope that photographs the entire sky in 102 colours of infrared light. Then it does it again, every six months.

Why infrared?

Infrared is light with a longer wavelength than our eyes can see. Cool things glow in it: brown dwarfs, clouds of dust, giant stars near the end of their lives. The light of very distant galaxies arrives stretched into it. And molecules such as water, carbon dioxide and methane each absorb infrared light at their own wavelengths, so an object's infrared colours reveal what it is made of.

102 colours without a filter wheel

Most cameras take one colour at a time. SPHEREx looks through filters whose colour changes smoothly from one edge to the other. As the telescope steps across the sky, each star lands on a different part of the filter in every exposure, and is recorded in a different colour.

Collect enough exposures of one star and you have its whole spectrum. That is why, in Chronosphere, stepping through time and building a spectrum are the same act.

What it was built for

  • The first instant of the universe. By mapping more than 450 million galaxies in three dimensions, it tests ideas about what happened in the first moment after the Big Bang.
  • The history of galaxies. It measures the combined glow of all galaxies, including ones too faint to see one by one.
  • Water and the ingredients of life. It surveys frozen water, carbon dioxide and other ices in the clouds where stars and planets form.

And what it does along the way

Because SPHEREx returns to every spot of the sky again and again, it also records everything that moves or changes: asteroids, comets, nearby stars drifting past, brown dwarfs, stars that pulse or flare. It photographs hundreds of millions of stars and galaxies, far more than any one team can check. This tool exists so that more people can look.
